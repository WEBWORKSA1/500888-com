Why 4 is unlucky: tetraphobia explained

Missing floors, re-numbered phones and discounted homes — the economics of the world's most avoided digit.

By the 500888 Editorial Team · Updated 1 October 2026

On this page

  2. Missing floors

Sounds like death

Four is sì (四); death is sǐ (死). Only the tone differs, and in everyday speech the association is unavoidable. The fear of four — tetraphobia — is shared across Chinese-, Japanese- and Korean-speaking communities.

Missing floors

Many residential towers in mainland China and Hong Kong skip the 4th floor, and often 14, 24 and other 4-floors too. Some Hong Kong towers, such as Vista Paradiso and The Arch, jump straight from 39 to 50, omitting the entire 40s. Hospitals and hotels frequently do the same.

What it means for business

Research on Vancouver home sales found that addresses ending in 4 sold at roughly a 2.2% discount, concentrated in neighbourhoods with many Chinese residents. For retailers, a 4 in a gift price — or a 4-piece gift set — can quietly suppress sales. Audit prices, SKU bundles, phone numbers and unit numbers.
