Lucky-number pricing: the playbook

What the research says, a 6-step audit, and the mistakes that quietly cost sales.

Why price endings matter

Western retail uses “charm prices” ending in 9 or 99. In Chinese-speaking markets the cultural charm digit is 8, with 6 and 9 close behind — while 4 is avoided. Customers may not consciously notice, but the signal is familiar and positive.

What the research shows

  • Advertising: Simmons & Schindler (2003, Journal of International Marketing) found Chinese price advertisements favour the ending digit 8 and avoid 4.
  • Cross-cultural pricing: Westjohn et al. (2017, Journal of International Marketing) confirm lucky-number pricing effects in Eastern markets.
  • Real estate: in Vancouver, addresses ending in 8 sold at a 2.5% premium, those ending in 4 at a 2.2% discount.
  • Listings: Canadian homes have been listed at prices like $888,888 specifically to appeal to Chinese buyers.

The 6-step lucky pricing audit

  1. Remove every 4 — in prices, bundle sizes, SKU counts and gift-set quantities.
  2. Re-anchor hero products on 8, 88, 98, 168 or 188 endings.
  3. Use .88 / .68 cents on low-ticket items (e.g. 19.88).
  4. Price gift sets in even numbers; use pairs (2, 6, 8) for set sizes.
  5. Check combinations — avoid 250, 514, 74 anywhere in a price.
  6. Time promotions around CNY, 5/20, Qixi, Mid-Autumn and 8/8 — and avoid the Ghost Festival for launches.

Costly mistakes

A $14 or $44 price point; a 4-pack gift box; a white or green-hat-themed gift (green hats imply infidelity); clocks as gifts (送钟 sounds like attending a funeral); launching on Qingming or the Ghost Festival.
