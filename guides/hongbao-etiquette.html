Red envelope (hongbao) etiquette

What a hongbao is

A hongbao (红包) — lai see in Cantonese — is a red envelope of money given at New Year, weddings, birthdays and other happy occasions. Red symbolises luck and wards off evil; the money is a wish for prosperity.

Who gives to whom

At New Year, married adults give to children and unmarried younger relatives; working adults also give to parents and grandparents; employers give to staff. At weddings, guests give to the couple.

How much to give

Customs vary by family, city and income. Common guidance seen in China (in RMB) and widely adapted abroad:

The unwritten rules

  • Even amounts for happy occasions; odd amounts in white envelopes for funerals.
  • Never include a 4; avoid 250.
  • Use new, crisp notes — no coins.
  • Give and receive with both hands; open later, not in front of the giver.
  • At weddings, aim to at least cover your seat at the banquet.

Digital red packets

WeChat and Alipay made red packets digital — including “grab” packets in group chats where random amounts are split among members. The same number etiquette applies: 8.88, 66.66 or 168 are popular amounts.
