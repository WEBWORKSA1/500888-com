It's all in the sound

In Mandarin, eight is bā (八). It sounds close to fā (发) — as in 发财 fācái, “to make a fortune”. In Cantonese the match is even closer: eight is baat and prosper is faat. Because Chinese has many homophones, numbers inherit the meaning of the words they resemble. That single sound-alike is why 8 became the most sought-after digit in the Chinese-speaking world.

Repeat it and the wish multiplies: 88 is double prosperity (and, in internet slang, “bye-bye”), and 888 is 发发发, “prosper, prosper, prosper”.

From folk belief to national stage

The most famous demonstration came in 2008: the Beijing Summer Olympics opening ceremony began at 8:08:08 pm on 8 August 2008 (08/08/08) local time. Eight also appears in classical culture — the Eight Immortals (八仙) and the Eight Trigrams (八卦) of the I Ching — reinforcing its sense of completeness and good fortune.

How to use 8 well

For prices, end on 8, 88 or 98. For phone numbers, the last four digits matter most. For gifts, even amounts with 8s are ideal — but never pair them with a 4. And remember dialect: some combinations read differently in Cantonese and Mandarin. When in doubt, run it through the analyzer.
