How Chinese number luck works

Chinese is a tonal language with many homophones, so numbers borrow the meaning of the words they sound like. 8 (bā) sounds like 发 (fā), “to prosper”. 6 (liù) echoes 流 (liú), “to flow smoothly”. 9 (jiǔ) echoes 久 (jiǔ), “long-lasting”. 4 (sì) sounds close to 死 (sǐ), “death”.

Combinations read like short phrases: 168 = 一路发 (“prosperity all the way”), 518 = 我要发 (“I will prosper”), 520 = 我爱你 (“I love you”). Unlucky ones include 14 (要死), 74 (气死) and 250 (slang for “idiot”).

FAQ

How is the luck score calculated?

Each digit gets a cultural weight from its homophone (8 = prosper, 6 = smooth, 9 = lasting, 4 = death). The final digit counts 1.5×. Famous combinations such as 168, 518, 888 and 1314 add points; combinations like 14, 74, 250 and 514 subtract. The total is normalised to 1–100.

Does it work for letters?

Letters are ignored — only digits are scored. For plates and domains, enter the full text and we'll extract the numbers.

Mandarin or Cantonese?

The core meanings are shared, and we flag the main differences — for example 5 reads as 'I' in Mandarin wordplay but can sound like 'not' in Cantonese, and 28 is especially lucky in Cantonese.

Can a high score make me rich?

No number guarantees anything. But perception is real: research shows Chinese-speaking buyers respond to 8s and avoid 4s, which affects prices, demand and resale value.
